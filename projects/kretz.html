Where it started

I have spent ten years in sports: six as a player, six as a coach and two on a club board as deputy leader responsible for the finances. Running a club's economy meant juggling spreadsheets, separate payment tools and a lot of manual follow-up done by volunteers.

Integrations

A club's money already lives in other systems: the accounting software the treasurer uses, the app members pay with, and the sports federation's member register. Kretz is only useful if it works with those systems instead of adding one more place to type the same numbers. That makes integrations the core of what I'm working on now.

  • Accounting first. Clubs keep their books in different systems, so Kretz connects to the three that treasurers use: Tripletex, Fiken and Uni Micro. When what happens in Kretz flows straight into the books, nobody has to enter it twice.
  • Payments next. Vipps is how members already pay, so connecting it means fees and payments land in Kretz without manual matching.

What I build

I built the frontend of Kretz: the website and the admin application clubs work in every day. Below is how it's put together, first from the technical side and then from the finance side.

How it's built

  • Component-based interface. Every screen is assembled from reusable components such as KPI cards, data tables, status pills, forms and calendars. The finance pages, booking and week plan share one design system, so they look and behave the same way.
  • Multi-tenant structure. One administrator can manage several clubs, and every view can be filtered by club, sport and group. Roles such as chair, treasurer and coach are assigned per club and team.
  • Data-heavy views. Transaction tables with full-text search across fields, period filters (this year, this month, custom range) and export, plus charts for category breakdowns and monthly results with hover details.
  • Interactive tools. Drag and drop in the week plan with an explicit save-and-publish step, a booking flow with real-time availability per pitch and automatic member pricing, and QR-code check-in for volunteer days.
  • API integrations. I'm connecting Kretz to the APIs of Tripletex, Fiken and Uni Micro, so transactions flow between Kretz and the club's books instead of being entered twice.

The finance behind it

  • Cash position. Team fund consolidates balances across bank accounts and shows inflows, outflows, opening balance, net change and closing balance for any period: a simple cash flow statement.
  • Receivables and payables. Member invoicing covers membership fees the club is owed (accounts receivable), and supplier invoices cover what the club owes (accounts payable). Unpaid invoices are flagged on the home screen.
  • Income statement by month. Key figures breaks revenue down by source (membership fees, sponsors, events, kiosk, grants) and costs by type, and plots the monthly result against income and expenses.
  • Financial ratios. Profit margin, liquidity ratio, equity ratio, fee collection rate and cost per member, each with a status such as healthy, watch or below target.
  • Funding pipeline. Grants with amount ranges and deadlines, and sponsor agreements with contract periods, renewal dates and reporting obligations, so the club's external revenue is tracked like a pipeline.

The people using it are mostly volunteers, not accountants. The goal is that a treasurer can read the club's financial health at a glance, without knowing how the bookkeeping works underneath.
